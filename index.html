About Blood Donation

Why India Urgently Needs Blood Donors

India collects approximately 11 million units of blood annually, falling short of the 14 million units required. This deficit is most acute during natural disasters, road accidents, and for patients with conditions like thalassaemia and cancer.

  • You must be 18–65 years old and weigh at least 50 kg
  • Men can donate every 3 months; women every 4 months
  • The process is sterile — new disposable needles are used every time
  • Your body replenishes donated blood within 24–48 hours
  • Blood is separated into red cells, plasma, and platelets — helping multiple patients
  • Voluntary, non-remunerated donation is the safest form of blood supply

Why Donate

Benefits Beyond the Donation Chair

According to The Lancet Journal, India has the world’s biggest blood shortage, with a total shortfall of 41 million units and demand outstripping supply by over 400 percent.



Donating blood is good for the recipient — and surprisingly good for you too. Here's why thousands of Indians donate regularly.

Reduces Heart Risk

Regular donation lowers iron levels and reduces the risk of heart disease and stroke.

Free Health Check

Every donation includes a free haemoglobin, BP, pulse, and blood group check by trained staff.

Stimulates New Blood Cells

Donating triggers your bone marrow to produce fresh blood cells, keeping your system healthy.

Emotional Fulfilment

Knowing your blood has saved a child's life or helped a trauma patient is profoundly uplifting.

Calorie Burn

Donating 450 ml of blood burns approximately 650 calories — a small bonus for your health.

Recognition & Rewards

Regular donors receive certificates, priority care benefits, and recognition in their community.
